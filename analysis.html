Findings

  1. Free entry is the norm, free cash is not. 193 rows claim a free path, but only 133 state a cash amount at all, and 80 rows show “—” on this site because the source either states no figure or cites no official source.
  2. The dataset is seasonal and it shows. 27 rows are out of season, almost all of them sports brackets and pick'em contests. Any "best of" list that ignores status is misleading within weeks.
  3. Effort is inversely related to certainty. Pick'em contests are low-effort with tiny per-entrant odds and a tie-breaker; tech and forecasting contests are high-effort with objective scoring and far fewer entrants. For an AI-assisted entrant, the second shape is worth more per hour.
  4. Non-cash prizes hide in plain sight. Gems, tickets, SB points, trading credits and "funded accounts" all appear as prizes. They are not dollars, and we say so on every such row.
  5. Sourcing quality varies wildly. 11 rows have no official-tier source at all, and 117 "rules" links are just the signup page. A prize claim that cannot be read on an operator page is a rumour.
  6. Referral codes are an incentive, not information. 12 rows carried codes; none is republished here.

What this data cannot tell you

  • Odds of winning. No entrant counts, no historical win rates, no field sizes. The ledger is a catalogue of contests, not a record of outcomes.
  • Expected value per hour. Deadlines, effort and skill ceilings are not fields in the dataset. We can rank prize size and entry friction; we cannot rank your personal EV.
  • Whether a contest is running this week. The data is 74 days old and only 13 rows were re-fetched. Status is the source's label, not a live calendar.
  • Legality in your jurisdiction. Some rows are skill-cash games restricted by US state, others are UK/Ireland-only. Read the operator's eligibility section.
  • Tax treatment. We only record that US prizes of $600+ normally trigger a W-9. That is context from the source, not advice.
