What the evidence supports

Every statement below is derived from the 212 rows in data/entries.json or from an official page read on 2026-11-01. Where a number is a judgement rather than a fact, it is labelled.

2. Where the money actually is

Documented prizes span five orders of magnitude. The largest verified pools are technical (ARC Prize 2026: $2,000,000 across three tracks; GEMS Prize Challenge: $300,000; Kaggle’s featured competitions, where the ARC paper track alone showed $450,000). None of those can be won in an afternoon — they are research projects with open-source obligations.

The best ratio of prize to effort, for someone competing now, is in judgement games: ESPN NFL Pick’em pays $102,000 across 58 prizes and costs nothing to enter; Metaculus pays forecasting accuracy from pools that reach $50,000. The best creative options are the Wergle Flomp humor poetry contest ($5,100, explicitly no fee) and the Sony World Photography Awards (free entry, $25,000 overall Series prize).

3. Free entry has to be checked, not assumed

Six checked rows explicitly state free entry, one states a fee, and eleven do not state either. The reference ledger listed the Tom Howard/Margaret Reid poetry contest as a free cash lead; its own page charges $27 per submission. Assumption is what produces that kind of error, so blanks stay blank.

Two other traps are visible in the data. First, "free" rooms in skill-game apps pay in gems or bonus cash — the twelve skill-tournament leads are all paid-entry cash rooms or non-cash free rooms. Second, a prize pool is not a prize: Metaculus lists tournaments with "NO PRIZE POOL" alongside $50,000 ones.

4. The link problem on the reference site

25 leads point at a news article or blog post rather than a rules page, and 4 URLs are reused across several different contests. The most reused is fantasy.premierleague.com, linked for eight separate rows. A single site URL cannot document eight distinct cash prizes, and none of those eight rows has a prize read from an official rules page. Challenge.gov, linked from the HeroX row, was retired on 2026-03-30 and now serves a sunset notice; USA.gov hosts the official replacement directory and is used here instead.

5. How to use an AI coding agent on these competitions

  • Pick a contest with a published, machine-checkable rule set. Kaggle and DrivenData publish metric, data and submission format, so an agent can iterate without guessing.
  • Respect the automation boundary. ARC Prize states that internet access is not available during Kaggle evaluation, so any agent must run offline inside the submission environment. Metaculus scoring is about calibration, not code speed.
  • Budget the compliance work, not just the modelling. Open-source licences, write-ups and reproducibility requirements are eligibility conditions at ARC Prize, not optional extras.
  • Never let an agent enter, deposit or accept terms on your behalf. Eligibility rules, tax forms and identity checks are personal and usually binding.
  • Time-box by deadline. 10 verified rows have a future deadline; the nearest is ARC Prize (via Kaggle) on 2026-11-02 submissions due; 2026-11-08 papers due; 2026-12-04 results.

6. Editorial triage (labelled as judgement)

The scores on the overview page are arithmetic on verified facts plus two editorial inputs stored in data/triage.json with a written rationale per row: the size of the documented prize (0–3) and the effort needed for a credible entry (low/medium/high). No probability, expected value, win rate or time-to-win figure is invented anywhere on this site, because none of the official pages read publishes one.

7. Cross-check with the second independent pass

A parallel session verified 12 rows separately and stored verbatim quotes in data/verification.json, printed in full on the method page. The two passes agree on the facts they share: Wergle Flomp is free with $5,100 in prizes, Tom Howard charges $27 and is closed, ESPN is free with $102,000, ARC pays $2,000,000 and closes on 2026-11-02, and Metaculus is free with pools that include zero. Where the two passes differ, the difference is published rather than averaged: that pass marked the Weather Photographer of the Year prize ladder as not reproducible from the operator page, which is why this site leaves that prize cell blank.

8. What would change the picture

  • Confirming whether the 25 news-linked leads still have live rules pages.
  • Re-checking the February 2026 Papaya and Skillz terms for cash-room geography, since both change state lists frequently.
