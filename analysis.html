2. AI-agent compatibility: what the rules actually say

Allowed or required

  • Codex x TwitchCon: "Participants may use standard development tools, including frameworks, libraries, starter templates, and AI coding assistants." Official rules ↗
  • PayPal AI Hackathon: "along with an AI tool, model, or platform of their choice". Official rules ↗
  • Global Virtual Hackathon: "Building with AI tools is allowed and expected." Official page ↗
  • Metaculus FutureEval: "a bot-only competition where bot-makers attempt to push AI to its limits in predicting future events." Official page ↗
  • GEMS Prize official rules §3.2: "Using generative AI technology in the development of your prize submission is allowed. However, you must indicate in the narrative ... the extent to which, if any, you used generative AI technology and how you used it". Official rules ↗

Restricted or prohibited

  • Metaculus platform rules: "Unless otherwise specifically permitted in the Competition-specific rules, automated systems, bots, artificial intelligence, or user accounts which are officially registered as bot accounts, are ineligible for tournament prizes. However, human participants are allowed to use AI tools or systems to assist in their forecasting, as long as the final submissions reflect their own decisions and understanding." Official rules ↗ The bot-only FutureEval tournament is a competition-specific exception, not a general permission.
  • Winning Writers: "may not contain any AI-generated text". Official page ↗
  • ESPN: "Entries must be manually key-stroked by the entrant; use of automated entry devices or programs, or entries by third parties are prohibited." Official rules ↗
  • CBS: may disqualify "accumulating points through methods such as automated computer scripts". Official rules ↗
  • ARC Prize: "Internet access is not available during Kaggle evaluation (no API-based systems like GPT/Claude/etc.)" and code must be open source. Official page ↗
  • TradingView: scripts with excessively high transaction activity "will lead to a ban". Official rules ↗

Reading: an AI coding agent is a genuine fit for objective-scoring tech/forecasting events whose rules invite it, and a liability in pick'em or judged-writing contests. Metaculus is the clearest example of a split rule: AI assistance is allowed for human forecasts, but bots cannot win prizes by default — only a competition with explicit permission (such as FutureEval) changes that. This is analysis of the quoted rules, not a claim about any operator's enforcement. See R-18 and R-17.

5. Findings

  1. Confirmed free entry is common; confirmed free cash is narrower. 17 rows quote a free/no-purchase statement, 24 quote a prize figure, and 1 quote a fee. Non-withdrawable Bonus Cash, Gems and SB points are labelled as such.
  2. Headline figures need their wording. MLB's $5,600,000 is a 57-streak annuity ($3,000,000 lump sum); CBS's $100,000 is a perfect-week contingency; PayPal's $67,500 total includes Render credits. We publish the wording, not a single invented number.
  3. The data is seasonal and it shows. ESPN Survivor entry has closed, FOX Super 6 has no active games, Tom Howard is closed and TradingView's July edition ended 14 August 2026 — each confirmed on the operator's own page.
  4. Sourcing quality is uneven. 11 rows cite only forums, blogs or old articles. Their values are blank, not guessed.
  5. Verification is deliberately incomplete. 191 of 221 rows were not re-read on 2 Nov 2026. That gap is published rather than hidden.

6. What this evidence cannot tell you

  • Odds of winning, field sizes, historical win rates and expected value per hour.
  • Whether a contest is running beyond the moment its page was read on 2 Nov 2026.
  • Legality in your jurisdiction, identity requirements, and the tax treatment of a prize.
  • The current value of any figure on the 191 rows we did not re-read.
