Executive summary

What the evidence actually supports

Prepared 2026-11-01 UTC. This page is the short version: what was verified, which opportunities look worth the time, what is still unknown, and what could go wrong. Every claim links to the page it came from.

1. The problem this rebuild solves

The reference catalogue at karagemop466-tech.github.io/FreeMoneyApps lists 206 competitions on a single page and states: “No search box, no filters: just scroll.” On a phone that is a long, undifferentiated scroll through hundreds of blocks with no way to sort by deadline or see which entries are actually verified. Several of its links are dead: six of its source domains no longer resolve, one row is only reachable over plain HTTP, and one source is a 2024 news article. Its prize figures are all stamped 2026-08-19 and none is re-established against a current operator rules page.

2. What was done

  • 205 legacy listings preserved, with names and categories imported but every prize, fee and deadline field left blank. The legacy claim is displayed only in a column explicitly labelled unverified.
  • 44 rows verified by opening the operator's own page on 2026-11-01 and reading the prize, entry cost, deadline or eligibility from it. Each row links that page and stamps the check date.
  • 17 irregularities flagged and published on the Flagged for review page with sources, instead of being silently fixed or dropped.
  • Mobile-first rebuild: a dashboard, per-category pages, search, filters, sorting and card layouts, with no build step needed to view it.

3. Where the verified money is

After checking, the shape of the market is clear. Free entry with real cash is concentrated in tech, data and forecasting — the categories where the deliverable is a model, a paper or an app. Sports pick'em is genuinely free and pays cash, but the prizes are diluted across tens of thousands of entrants. Most skill-game and trivia listings from the legacy catalogue are not free cash paths at all: the free rooms pay gems or bonus currency, and the cash rooms charge an entry fee.

4. What is not known

  • No win probabilities exist in any inspected source. This site therefore publishes no expected value, hourly rate or “best odds” ranking. Any site that does is inferring.
  • Four categories have no verified row (skill, market, pools, directories). They are labelled as a coverage gap rather than filled.
  • Most hackathon prize totals are stated “in prizes”, not “in cash”. The cash/non-cash split is unknown for those rows and is flagged.
  • External reachability is not certified. DNS was checked for all 223 legacy hostnames; individual deep links were spot-checked, not exhaustively fetched.

5. Recommended next steps

  1. Work the two code-heavy tracks with near-term deadlines first: the shortlist sorted by deadline puts ARC Prize, PayPal AI and GEMS at the top.
  2. Treat forecasting tournaments as background work — they are free, they reward calibration, and a bot tournament with a $50,000 pool is explicitly built for automated approaches.
  3. Before any submission, re-open the rules page from the row and confirm (a) entry is still free, (b) you are eligible by country and age, (c) the deadline in your time zone. Rules changed mid-season on at least two rows checked here.
  4. Review the flagged items and tell us which leads to verify next; that list is ordered by evidence value, not by prize size.

6. Limits of this document

Verification is a snapshot: a page read on 2026-11-01 can change tomorrow. Cash prizes may carry tax consequences (US reporting commonly starts at $600, but confirm with the operator and a tax professional). Nothing here is financial, legal or tax advice, and no operator paid for a listing.
