## 3. Irregularities (published with source links on `review.html`)

1. **Retired link still in the ledger.** The HeroX row's source, `https://www.challenge.gov/`, now serves a sunset notice ("The Challenge.gov platform has been sunset", sunset date **2026-03-30**) and redirects readers to USA.gov. Row updated to <https://www.usa.gov/challenges>; the original URL is recorded in the row's flag.
2. **"Free" contest charging a fee.** Tom Howard/Margaret Reid Poetry: the operator page states "Fee: $27 per submission". The ledger presents it as a free cash lead. Marked *Paid entry*, excluded from ranking, flagged.
3. **One URL, many contests.** `fantasy.premierleague.com` is the source for 8 rows; `fantasyfootballscout.co.uk` for 4; `fantasy.espn.com` for 14 (those are distinct game pages, so the reuse is legitimate there). A single non-rules URL cannot document several prize offers.
4. **News articles as prize evidence.** 25 rows link to a path containing `/news/`, `/blog/` or an article slug instead of a rules or signup page.
5. **No usable operator link (2 rows).** `thelines-survivor` and `squarespool`. A non-operator article and an HTTP-only page were deliberately not substituted.
6. **Operator contradicts itself (ESPN).** "Max Entries 10" vs "up to five total" on the same page. Both readings recorded; no silent choice made.
7. **Non-contest listed as a contest (Givling).** Payout depends on a crowdfunding queue and random Spinners, and the ledger's August status said `ended` while the operator site is active.
8. **Stale August labels.** 27 rows `ended`, 5 `mixed`, 11 `caution`, 2 blank, stamped 2026-08-19. None is repeated as current fact; all 192 unverified rows show as *Unverified*.
9. **Paid-entry and non-cash categories retained.** The 12 skill-tournament leads are paid cash rooms or non-cash free rooms; the 7 pool leads are participant-funded tools; 19 market-simulation leads are largely broker marketing. Kept visible and labelled, never ranked.
10. **Headline prize is not cash.** The PayPal AI Hackathon advertises $67,500 cash / $69,750 total, while the rules list individual awards including non-cash credits. No prize amount is shown for that row.
11. **Half-verified hackathon rows.** The Codex x TwitchCon rules section read does not include 1st and 2nd place amounts, so only the amounts actually seen are shown.
12. **JavaScript-only pages.** Topcoder, Zindi and Reedsy entry lists would not render server-side; they stay unverified rather than being read from partial output.

## 4. Method limits

- Reading a page proves what that page said on 2026-11-01, not that a contest is open, that you qualify, or that a prize will be paid.
- Prize pools change; Kaggle and Metaculus figures in particular move as events open and close.
- Eligibility summaries are shortened from longer rules; the linked page wins.
- No probability, expected value, win rate or time-to-win estimate is published, because no official source read provides one.
- Not legal, financial or tax advice. US federal tax reporting generally starts at $600 in winnings for a calendar year, but each person's obligation is their own.
