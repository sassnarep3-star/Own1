misleading prize

Broker 'demo contests' pay trading bonuses, not cash

HFM's official FAQ answers 'Are the prizes real cash?' with 'No, the prizes are trading bonuses that will be credited to your live trading account.' The other brokers in the market-simulations category make similar offers, so none of them should be read as a cash prize until their own terms confirm it.

Sources: HFM Virtual to Real demo contest FAQ

not free

The reference ledger mixed paid-entry and bonus-credit offers into a page about free money. Two verified examples: the Tom Howard/Margaret Reid poetry contest charges $27 per submission, and Papaya's cash tournaments are funded by entry fees (free rooms pay Gems, not dollars). Both are kept with a 'paid' or 'not free' label instead of being recommended.

Sources: Tom Howard/Margaret Reid contest page ('Fee: $27 per submission') · Papaya FAQ (entry-fee funded cash tournaments)

AI restriction

Some free contests ban AI-generated entries outright

Winning Writers contests state that submitted writing 'must be composed using your own words, and may not contain any AI-generated text'. An AI agent can help you find and plan an entry, but not write it. The Prize Fest photo rules take the same position on generated images.

Sources: Wergle Flomp contest rules (AI policy) · Prize Fest Photo Prize rules ('AI generation tools... strictly prohibited')

internal contradiction

ESPN's own How-to-Play text says players 'can now create up to 10 entries', while the FAQ on the same page says 'you can create another entry (up to five total)'. Nobody should plan around either number without checking the live entry screen.

Sources: ESPN NFL Pick'em How to Play / FAQ
