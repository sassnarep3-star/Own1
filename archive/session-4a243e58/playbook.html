Playbook

How to spend your time — and how an AI agent helps

This page is strategy, not evidence. It is clearly separated from the tables because it is judgement. Every factual claim still links to an official source.

1. Filter before you fall in love

Four questions, in this order. Fail one and the prize does not matter:

  • Am I eligible? Age, country, US state or territory carve-outs, and student-only divisions. ESPN voids Puerto Rico and Quebec; the Codex hackathon excludes Brazil and Quebec; Metaculus prizes depend on the individual tournament rules.
  • Is entry actually free? A "free to enter" catalogue entry can still charge per submission — the Tom Howard poetry contest charges $27. Check the fee line on the organiser's page, not a listicle.
  • Is AI/automation allowed? Winning Writers prohibits AI-written entries. ARC Prize bans internet access during Kaggle evaluation. Codex x TwitchCon explicitly permits AI coding assistants. Getting this wrong wastes the whole effort and can void an entry.
  • Is it still open? Read the deadline on the organiser's page. Several catalogue links point at closed editions.

2. Where an agent actually earns its keep

An agent is strong at bounded, verifiable work and weak at judged originality. That maps onto the categories like this:

CategoryWhyBest use of the agent
AI, data & codeObjective scoring, published rules, reproducible evaluation.Feature engineering, model search, leak checks, notebook hygiene, writing the submission write-up.
ForecastingScored on calibration against resolvable questions.Base-rate research, question deconstruction, keeping a probability log. Bot tournaments exist for exactly this.
CreativityJudged subjectively, and often AI-restricted.Editing, proofreading, formatting, deadline tracking — and only where the rules allow that much.
Sports & marketsMostly luck over a short sample; the edge is small and the vig is real.Tracking picks, checking eligibility, avoiding paid-entry traps. Do not expect the agent to predict games.

3. The fit score, in plain words

Verified entries get a score so the shortlist is reproducible rather than vibes:

  • +40 confirmed free entry, +20 confirmed cash prize.
  • +20 / +12 / +4 for high / medium / low AI-agent fit.
  • +10 / +6 / +3 for low / medium / high effort.
  • +10 if the deadline is within 14 days, +6 within 60 days, +3 if no deadline is published, 0 if it has passed.

Consequence worth knowing: an event that never publishes a fee scores lower than a $27 contest that does. That is deliberate — the score rewards confirmed facts, not attractive ones. Read the entry page before dismissing anything.

4. Money, tax and the rules of the game

  • Nobody legitimate asks you to pay a fee to release a prize. Up-front payment to "unlock winnings" is a scam pattern.
  • Prize income in the US is taxable, and the reporting form depends on the type of income and the circumstances — the reference site's blanket "$600+" rule is not a legal rule. See IRS Publication 525 and Topic 419.
  • Skill-game apps that pay cash usually prohibit multi-accounting and often require identity verification before withdrawal. Read the terms before depositing anything.
  • "Free" practice currencies (gems, tickets, bonus cash) are not withdrawable money. Papaya's own FAQ distinguishes free Gem competitions from cash competitions.
  • Open-source requirements are common in AI prize competitions. ARC Prize makes open-sourcing a condition of eligibility.

5. A week that is actually worth it

  1. Open the ranked shortlist and take the top three that you are eligible for.
  2. For each, read the official rules page end to end once. Note the fee, the deadline, the AI policy and the eligibility carve-outs.
  3. Pick one. Competitions with a published, objective metric (Kaggle, DrivenData, ARC) reward a focused agent-assisted build; judged creative prizes rarely do.
  4. Set a stop rule before you start: the number of hours you will spend regardless of the leaderboard.
  5. When it closes, record what happened here. The next version of this ledger should be built from outcomes, not only from promises.
